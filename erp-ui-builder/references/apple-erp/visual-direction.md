# Visual direction

Use this reference after workflow correctness, hierarchy, accessibility, and lifecycle behavior are understood. The goal is not to make Hesabyar look fashionable; the goal is to make each surface feel intentional, premium, and appropriate to the work.

## Choose a direction before polishing

For a significant page or redesign, write one short visual thesis before styling. Choose the direction that best matches the task:

- **Calm / precise** — finance, accounting, approvals, audit, settings. Quiet surfaces, exact alignment, restrained emphasis, strong typography.
- **Dense / professional** — ledgers, stock, reconciliation, reports, operational lists. High information density with disciplined grouping and stable scanning.
- **Operational / industrial** — manufacturing, warehouse, fulfillment, fleet-like workflows. Strong state visibility, timelines, progress, exceptions, and direct actions.
- **Premium / minimal** — executive overview, party 360, high-level summaries. More breathing room and fewer simultaneous controls, without hiding evidence.
- **Fast / transactional** — POS, cashier, repetitive entry. Immediate feedback, stable targets, keyboard/touch efficiency, minimum decoration.

Do not mix several directions on one page. A page may inherit the product's overall language while emphasizing one task-specific direction.

## Visual thesis

Before implementation, state:

1. What should the user notice first?
2. What should the page feel like: calm, fast, exact, operational, investigative, or decision-oriented?
3. What is the one visual idea that makes this page memorable?
4. Which parts must remain deliberately quiet so the primary idea stays strong?

If the answers are unclear, do not invent styling yet.

## Product fit

The direction must:
- reuse existing Hesabyar tokens, Persian typography, module tones, and canonical components;
- preserve the established density and behavior of the closest workflow;
- adapt to real content, long Persian labels, large amounts, errors, loading, and empty states;
- remain coherent in light and dark themes;
- survive responsive/mobile composition without losing its identity.

## Restraint

A premium interface is usually quieter than a generated mockup:
- one accent role at a time;
- one dominant visual idea;
- one clear primary action;
- few elevation levels;
- few radius roles;
- no decorative container around every section;
- no gradient, blur, illustration, or animation unless it supports hierarchy, meaning, or product identity.

Visual novelty must never move a familiar ERP control or weaken operational clarity.
