# Visual anti-patterns

Reject these defaults unless there is a concrete product reason.

## Card everywhere

Do not wrap every section, form group, metric, filter, and table in separate floating cards. Prefer spacing, alignment, section headings, and subtle dividers.

## Gradient as importance

Do not use gradients to manufacture hierarchy. Use typography, position, semantic color, and content relevance first.

## Oversized KPI wallpaper

Do not make numbers huge merely to create a dashboard aesthetic. A KPI must earn prominence through decision value and should include context such as comparison, target, trend, or exception when relevant.

## Decorative glass / blur

Do not use glassmorphism, blur, or transparency because it looks premium. Use material effects only when they establish a useful floating layer or spatial hierarchy and remain readable in both themes.

## Random radius

Do not mix many corner radii or make every object pill-shaped. Use existing radius tokens by component role.

## Border boxing

Do not outline every region. Too many borders destroy hierarchy. Use containment only where boundaries matter.

## Icon confetti

Do not add an icon to every label or heading. Icons are for recognition, action, status, or meaningful categorization.

## Accent-color competition

Do not use several strong accent colors in the same viewport. Semantic colors keep their meaning; module/brand color is restrained.

## Fake premium spacing

Do not create excessive empty space that forces operational users to scroll more or separates related fields/data.

## Hero sections in ERP

Do not add marketing-style hero areas, decorative slogans, oversized illustrations, or giant greeting banners to operational pages.

## Generic generated-UI tropes

Avoid:
- gradient blobs;
- glowing borders;
- excessive shadows;
- unnecessary floating pills;
- every KPI as a colorful mini-card;
- arbitrary "01 / 02 / 03" numbering;
- decorative charts;
- large serif display type with no product reason;
- animated counters for routine operational values.

## Motion for spectacle

No looping decoration, parallax, bouncing actions, or delayed entrances in routine ERP work.

## Hidden complexity disguised as minimalism

A clean screenshot is not success if users lose access to provenance, status, totals, exceptions, permissions, or recovery.

When an aesthetic choice competes with scanability, integrity, or task speed, remove the aesthetic choice.
